My research focuses on atmospheric chemistry, air quality, volatile organic compounds (VOCs), biomass-burning emissions, and the application of advanced analytical instrumentation to field and laboratory measurements.

I am particularly interested in combining high-time-resolution chemical measurements with multi-instrument observations to understand emission sources, atmospheric composition, and air-quality impacts.

## Prescribed Fire Emissions

A major focus of my Ph.D. research is the characterization of gaseous and particulate emissions from prescribed fires in western U.S. forests.

I lead mobile-laboratory measurements that combine:

- Proton-transfer-reaction time-of-flight mass spectrometry (PTR-ToF-MS) for VOC measurements
- CO and CO₂ measurements
- NOx and ozone measurements
- PM₁ and PM₂.₅ measurements
- Black carbon and aerosol optical measurements
- Meteorological observations

These measurements are used to quantify emission ratios, emission factors, combustion characteristics, and variability among prescribed fires.

I am also interested in comparing prescribed-fire emissions with wildfire observations to better understand how fuel conditions, combustion efficiency, fire management practices, and atmospheric processing influence smoke composition.

## Volatile Organic Compounds and PTR-ToF-MS

I use PTR-ToF-MS to measure and characterize atmospheric VOCs from both natural and anthropogenic sources.

My work includes method development and quantitative characterization of volatile chemical product compounds such as:

- Benzyl alcohol
- Carbitol
- Isopropyl alcohol
- Linalool
- α-Terpineol

This work involves laboratory calibration, sensitivity characterization, analytical method evaluation, interference assessment, and uncertainty analysis.

I am particularly interested in improving the quantitative interpretation of PTR-ToF-MS measurements and applying these methods to atmospheric field observations.

## Ambient and Mobile Air-Quality Measurements

My research also includes long-term and mobile measurements of urban and regional air quality.

Projects include:

- Long-term analysis of ambient VOC measurements in Missoula, Montana
- Urban mobile air-quality profiling during the Salt Lake City Summer Ozone Study
- Analysis of VOC source patterns and environmental drivers
- Measurements of ozone, CO, NOx, black carbon, particulate matter, and meteorological variables

These datasets are used to investigate source attribution, atmospheric variability, and the factors controlling air-pollutant concentrations.

## Atmospheric Oxidation and Secondary Chemistry

I have contributed to laboratory and field studies examining atmospheric oxidation chemistry and secondary organic aerosol formation.

This work includes calibration and analysis of chamber VOC measurements as well as interpretation of oxidation products formed from volatile chemical product compounds.

I am interested in connecting primary emissions with their subsequent atmospheric processing and understanding how chemical transformation affects air quality.

## Instrumentation and Experimental Methods

A central part of my research is the operation, calibration, and troubleshooting of atmospheric measurement systems.

My experience includes:

- PTR-ToF-MS / PTR-MS
- GC-MS
- PAX black carbon instruments
- Ozone analyzers
- CO and CO₂ analyzers
- NOx analyzers
- PM₁ and PM₂.₅ instruments
- Meteorological sensors
- Mobile laboratory systems

I routinely perform:

- Instrument calibration
- Background correction
- QA/QC
- Data acquisition
- Cross-instrument comparison
- Uncertainty evaluation
- Field deployment and troubleshooting

My analysis work includes:

- Data cleaning and quality control
- Time alignment of multi-instrument measurements
- Statistical analysis and regression
- Visualization
- Emission-ratio calculations
- Emission-factor calculations
- Uncertainty analysis
- Literature comparison and benchmarking
- Analysis of large time-series datasets
